## Defense Conditions and Paired Evaluation

To avoid evaluating a mitigation only on examples that had already succeeded, we designed a paired, full-corpus comparison. Each of the 348 active attack test cases in dataset v2.1.0 is scheduled under three conditions: (D0) the standard scanner prompt without IPI-specific guidance; (D1) the standard prompt augmented with an instruction that treats webpage content as untrusted data and rejects attempts to alter the scan objective; and (D2) the same instruction-based guidance combined with an explicit structured boundary for fetched webpage observations. Under D2, page-derived observations are moved from the ordinary `pages` field into a separately named `untrusted_webpage_observations` array, with each item labelled `UNTRUSTED_WEBPAGE_DATA`; the trusted workflow requirements remain in a separate field.

The D0–D1 comparison measures the effect of adding instruction-based guidance. The D1–D2 comparison measures the incremental effect of structured context separation on top of the same guidance. D2 is therefore a combined defense condition, rather than a standalone test of structural labelling without instruction guidance. Across conditions, the model/provider configuration, attack content, crawler, target application, tool definitions, and local policy validator are held constant. The policy validator is not modified as part of either defense.

Each attack test case is repeated three times under each condition, yielding 3,132 planned attack runs (348 cases × 3 repetitions × 3 conditions). To measure whether a defense interferes with normal scanner operation, the 30 benign review fixtures introduced in the benign baseline protocol are also repeated three times under each condition, yielding 270 additional benign-utility runs. The complete primary schedule therefore contains 3,402 planned model calls. A single schedule is randomized using a prespecified seed and saved to a manifest before execution. The same case/fixture and repeat indices are paired across all conditions. Test-case IDs and fixture IDs are the primary descriptive units; repeated calls are nested observations and are not treated as independent samples.

The benign-utility outcome is defined operationally as the model requesting the expected `security_check` action on `/page`, the local policy validator allowing it, and the tool returning HTTP 200. This outcome measures completion of the predefined scanner workflow; it does not measure vulnerability-detection accuracy. For attack runs, the implementation records tool requests, expected-workflow deviations, policy blocks, tool executions, and execution failures. Final S0–S4 classifications, attack-success rates, and inferential comparisons are reported only after applying the annotation and analysis protocols specified separately.

## Limitations of the Defense Evaluation

The defense conditions are limited to instruction-level and context-representation changes in a single-step prototype. The structured-boundary condition combines explicit untrusted-content guidance with a separate representation for page-derived observations, so the incremental comparison with D1 does not establish the effectiveness of structural labelling in isolation. The evaluation uses one model configuration and one local target application. Three repetitions per case measure within-case variability but do not create independent attack samples. Accordingly, the results should be interpreted as a controlled comparison within this prototype rather than as a general estimate of defense effectiveness across models or commercial scanners.

## Reporting note

Do not include numerical findings in this subsection until the full scheduled runs are complete, the raw JSONL logs have been preserved, failed or missing jobs have been accounted for, and the later S0–S4 classification and metric protocol has been applied. The earlier 41-case success-selected subset and its 82.11-percentage-point difference must not be presented as an estimate of general defense effectiveness.
